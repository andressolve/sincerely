How to Start a Food Business in Port Coquitlam: A Complete Guide

February 2026 · 8 min read

← Back to Blog

Starting a food business is one of the most exciting things you can do — but it can also feel overwhelming. Between permits, health inspections, finding kitchen space, and actually producing your product, there's a lot to figure out.

The good news? It's simpler than you think, especially if you're in Port Coquitlam. This guide walks you through everything you need to know to go from home kitchen to licensed food business.

Step 1: Define Your Product and Business Model

Before anything else, get clear on what you're making and how you plan to sell it. Are you baking cakes for custom orders? Making hot sauce for retail? Preparing meals for delivery? Your business model affects everything from the permits you need to the kitchen hours you'll require.

Don't worry if your plan isn't perfect yet. Many of our members started with a rough idea and refined it over time.

Step 2: Get Your Business License

Every food business operating in Port Coquitlam needs a business license from the City. Here's what you'll need:

  • Your business name (you can register a sole proprietorship or incorporate)
  • A commercial kitchen address (this is where a shared kitchen like Sincerely Kitchen comes in)
  • A completed application form from the City of Port Coquitlam

The process typically takes 1-2 weeks. If you're working out of Sincerely Kitchen, we provide the commercial address and help you through the application.

Step 3: Get Fraser Health Approval

This is the permit that allows you to legally produce and sell food in BC. Fraser Health will require:

  • A completed food safety plan
  • Your FoodSafe Level 1 certificate (you can take the course online)
  • A site inspection of your production kitchen

The inspection is straightforward if you're working out of an already-approved commercial kitchen. At Sincerely Kitchen, our facility is already Fraser Health approved, which makes this step much easier for our members.

"Most of our members get fully approved in 4-6 weeks. We walk you through every step — the paperwork, the food safety plan, the inspection prep. It's all included with your membership."

Step 4: Find Your Kitchen Space

You can't legally produce food for sale in a residential kitchen in BC. You need access to a commercial kitchen. Your options include:

  • Build your own: $50,000-$100,000+ and 6-12 months of construction
  • Rent a dedicated space: High fixed costs before you have revenue
  • Use a shared commercial kitchen: Start from $490/month with everything included

A shared kitchen is the most practical option for most new food entrepreneurs. You get access to commercial equipment, storage, and a licensed facility without the massive upfront investment.

Step 5: Start Producing and Selling

Once you're licensed and have kitchen access, you're ready to go. Start small, test your market, and grow at your own pace. Common first steps include:

  • Selling at local farmers markets
  • Taking custom orders through social media
  • Approaching local cafes and retailers
  • Setting up online ordering and delivery

There's no single right path. Some of our members went from zero to retail distribution in a year. Others took a slower, steadier approach. Both are valid.

What It Costs to Get Started

Here's a realistic breakdown of your startup costs:

  • Business license: ~$150 (City of Port Coquitlam)
  • FoodSafe Level 1: ~$100 (online course)
  • Shared kitchen membership: From $490/month
  • Ingredients and packaging: Varies by product
  • Permit assistance: Free at Sincerely Kitchen

Compare that to $50,000+ to build your own kitchen, and you can see why shared kitchens have become the go-to for food entrepreneurs.
